## Texto — How artificial intelligence is shaping the future of maritime transport

**[1]** The maritime industry is facing one of the most significant transformations in its history. As global demand for energy and goods continues to grow, shipping companies are under increasing pressure to reduce their environmental footprint while maintaining operational efficiency. Artificial intelligence (AI) is emerging as a valuable tool in addressing these challenges. However, its widespread adoption will depend on more than technological advances alone.

**[2]** One of the most promising applications of AI is voyage optimization. Traditionally, shipping routes have been planned according to weather forecasts, fuel availability and estimated arrival times. Today, intelligent systems can process vast amounts of real-time data, allowing vessels to adjust their routes and speeds according to changing conditions. By doing so, shipping companies can cut down on fuel consumption without necessarily compromising delivery schedules.

**[3]** Predictive maintenance is another area in which digital technologies are making a considerable difference. Rather than waiting for equipment to fail, operators can use sensors and machine learning algorithms to identify potential problems before they occur. This approach not only extends the lifespan of critical equipment but also reduces unexpected downtime, which can be particularly costly for companies operating offshore.

**[4]** Ports are undergoing a similar transformation. Automated systems can help coordinate the arrival of vessels, manage cargo operations and reduce congestion. When ships arrive at a port before a berth is available, they may spend several hours waiting. Better coordination between ports and shipping companies could therefore lead to substantial energy savings.

**[5]** Despite these advantages, the increasing reliance on digital infrastructure brings new concerns. As vessels become more connected, they also become more vulnerable to cyber threats. A disruption in communication systems could affect navigation, cargo management and even the safety of crew members. Consequently, investment in cybersecurity must keep pace with technological development.

**[6]** Another major challenge is the availability of reliable data. Although large shipping companies may have sufficient financial resources to implement advanced technologies, smaller operators often struggle to afford them. Unless these barriers are addressed, digitalization could widen the gap between companies rather than benefit the industry as a whole.

**[7]** Human expertise will remain essential throughout this transition. AI can assist professionals in making informed decisions, but it cannot entirely replace human judgment, particularly in unexpected situations. Instead of eliminating the need for skilled workers, technological innovation is likely to change the nature of their responsibilities.

**[8]** Ultimately, the future of maritime transport will depend on the industry's ability to strike a balance between profitability, sustainability and safety. While alternative fuels and cleaner propulsion systems will play a crucial role in reducing emissions, digital solutions can deliver benefits even before these technologies become widely available. The challenge is not merely to adopt new tools, but to ensure that they are used effectively and responsibly.
